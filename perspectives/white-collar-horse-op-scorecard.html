Portfolio AI value-drift scorecard

One red-amber-green scorecard, run the same way across every company in the fund, so AI value-drift becomes comparable, rankable, and ready to put in front of the investment committee. Seven dimensions, drawn from the seven questions in the Operating Partner brief.

Cumai Aboul Housn, Iga Sloan and Alex Abbott

Supero · Updated 14 July 2026 · Print or fill on screen

The brief gives you the seven questions. This turns the answers into a single view you can compare across the portfolio and rank for the IC.

Score each company against the seven dimensions below. The example rows are illustrative — replace them with your own assets, or print this page and mark it up by hand in a portfolio review. The overall rating is worst-weighted on purpose: a single red in value intent, drift, or kill-switch authority pulls the company red, because that is where a quiet problem becomes an expensive one.

The seven dimensions

1. Value intent

Is AI tied to a named line in the value-creation plan, with evidence it is landing, not just running?

Green: growth/margin target named, owned, tracked. Amber: intent stated, no measurement. Red: AI running with no link to the plan.

2. Inventory & ownership

Is there a list of where AI shapes commercial decisions, each with a named human owner?

Green: complete list, owners named. Amber: partial or informal. Red: no inventory.

3. Drift check cadence

When was AI last checked for optimising to the ICP and strategy rather than to whatever converts fastest?

Green: checked within the quarter. Amber: over a quarter ago. Red: never checked.

4. Kill-switch & authority

How fast could an AI system be paused or withdrawn, and who has authority to sign it off?

Green: same-day, authority named. Amber: days, unclear owner. Red: no route to pause.

5. EU AI Act posture

Which systems sit in the risk classification, what oversight is required, ahead of or behind the deadlines?

Green: classified, ahead of deadlines. Amber: aware, work outstanding. Red: unclassified.

6. Diligence readiness

What could be evidenced in 48 hours on how AI shapes commercial decisions?

Green: documentation ready. Amber: reconstructable with effort. Red: nothing to show.

7. Usage policy

Is there a written AI-usage policy, and is there evidence people operate inside it?

Green: policy live and adopted. Amber: policy exists, adoption unknown. Red: no policy.

How to read it at the investment committee

  1. Rank by overall, then by value intent. A red on value intent is a company where AI may be running hard in the wrong direction — the most expensive kind of drift, and the hardest to see on a dashboard.
  2. Read the pattern down each column, not just across each row. If most of the portfolio is red on drift-check cadence or diligence readiness, that is a fund-level standard gap, not six separate company problems.
  3. Watch the assets nearing exit. Value creation concentrates in the final year of the hold; a company amber-to-red on value intent or diligence readiness within 18 months of exit is carrying that risk into the room where it costs the most.
  4. Turn each red into a value-creation-plan clause with an owner and a date, then re-score at the next quarterly review. The scorecard is only useful if the colours move.

Run it once, across the whole portfolio

Supero runs this diagnostic with each management team and hands the fund a ranked scorecard for the IC and a value-creation-plan clause for each asset.
